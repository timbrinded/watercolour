# Water: sea, surf, foam and wet sand

Read this before painting open water, waves breaking on a shore, foam, spray or a wet beach, whether from an idea or
from a photograph. Each passage of a beach needs its own method: the one that painted the sand well made the sea
muddy, and the one that painted the sea well lost the foam. This file says which method to use for which passage,
in what order, and what failed.

It was worked out on three beach paintings and about twenty rehearsal sheets (harness 0.7.1): one from an idea
(waves lapping on a calm beach, with lace foam), and two copies of a photograph of surf breaking on salmon-pink sand,
768 by 1024. The masking trials were run on 0.8.0. Where no masking fluid is laid, 0.8.0 paints exactly as 0.7.1
did (the same pass on the same seed gave identical pixels), so the figures below hold on both. They come from one
subject and one palette; take the methods as settled and check the numbers on a rehearsal sheet.

## Which method for which passage

| Passage | Method | How |
|---|---|---|
| Sand, a calm sea, any broad passage that changes slowly | A variegated wash of rows | Below |
| Choppy open water | A base wash of rows, then every trough as one tapered stroke | `scripts/trace_troughs.py` |
| The face of a wave | Its own smooth measured passage, laid before the foam | `scripts/crisp_layers.py --smooth-mix` |
| A mass of breaking foam | Crisp measured layers with the whites kept as paper | `scripts/crisp_layers.py` |
| Lace: separate strands of foam on water | Negative painting, each stroke sized to its distance from the nearest foam | Below |
| Whites a single wash must cross (0.8.0; fine ones 0.10.0) | Masking fluid; not needed with the crisp layers | `scripts/mask_lights.py` |
| Spray and streaks narrower than about 2 pixels in radius | Kept as paper by the layers round them, or on 0.10.0 masked first | No method puts them back afterwards |
| Reflections in wet sand | Not reached | Leave a smooth sheen gradient |

Order on the sheet: sand; sea base wash; troughs; wave face; foam layers; then any glaze over the sand. Lay the
broad, slowly changing passages first and let each later pass measure what the sheet still lacks.

## A variegated wash of rows (sand, calm water)

`flat_wash` of size 10 at full water, rows 1.25 times the size apart, laid in alternate directions at speed 300.
Split each row into segments of about 190 pixels, each with its own mix solved from the reference under it, so the
wash changes across the sheet as well as down it. Overlap neighbouring segments by 12 pixels: segments that only
abut left a dotted line of paper down every join. These rows were generated by a short script of the study's own
from the settings in this section; `measured_layers.py` lays rows of the same kind with a mop or a round
(`copying-a-reference.md`, step 4), but was not tried on the sand.

- **Depth.** A filled wash of these rows dries with an absorbance of about `0.77 * S^0.75` times the paint's
  absorption (`S` the total strength): about twice what a single stroke of a size 9 round lays from the same load.
  The first sand wash here was mixed for a single stroke and dried twice too dark.
- **The sand's colour.** Solve warm sand from burnt sienna, quinacridone rose and cobalt blue. The solver chose
  yellow ochre and dried maroon and dark: it reads the paints' absorption only, and ochre's scattering makes it
  paint differently from that.
- **Follow the shoreline.** Start the first row on the swash edge and let the rows flatten out over the first
  hundred and fifty pixels. Push the edge below the foam's lowest whites first, or the sand wash runs into them.
- **Calm or distant water** is the same wash, mixed from the reference blurred by 8 pixels, at 0.9 of its strength
  so the troughs have room on top.

## Choppy water: a base wash and traced troughs

Measured layers and shape strokes both failed on open chop. The thirteen measured stages dried too dark, with brown
muddy troughs (edge agreement 0.09), and shape strokes traced looping worms. Strokes sized to the shortfall in
patches dried as round blobs. What read as chop was a pale base wash and then every trough laid as one tapered
stroke, the way a painter lays it:

1. The base wash above at 0.9, `dry`.
2. `trace_troughs.py --darker 5 --gain 0.55`, paint, look after the `dry`.
3. `trace_troughs.py --darker 11 --gain 1.0` on the new look.

```bash
python scripts/trace_troughs.py reference.png troughs_a.json --sheet 768,1024 \
    --paints cobalt_blue,phthalo_green,burnt_sienna --look looks/0002_painting_color.png \
    --region 0,0,768,300 --darker 5 --gain 0.55 --preview troughs_a.png
```

On a rehearsal of the sea that read as chop, with a value gap of 0.058 and an edge agreement of 0.15: low, because
the photograph's chop is finer than any brush, but the sheet reads as water. On the full sheet the two passes came to
about 1,700 strokes. Keep `--region` to the open water. Over the surf it traces the foam's shadows as squiggles, and
over a wave's face it draws long zigzags across the streaks. The troughs come out a little more regular than the
photograph's.

## The surf band

The face of the wave and the foam are painted inside the surf band only: from the lower edge of the open water to
the swash edge on the sand. Make an image the size of the sheet, white over that band and black elsewhere, from
points read off `reference_grid.py`, and give it to `crisp_layers.py` as `--within`. Without it the layers also
paint the sand and the sea wherever those are a little lighter than the reference. Lay the sand's wash up to the swash
edge first, with the edge pushed below the foam's lowest whites. Give every script the photograph's own white as
`--white` (`copying-a-reference.md`, step 1); the commands below leave it out for brevity.

## The face of a wave

Paint the dark water under a breaking crest as its own passage, before the foam, not as whatever the foam layers
leave. Left to the layers it came out 0.06 to 0.08 too light and mottled brown-grey.

- **Its shape:** the reference darker than 0.55 in value inside the band, its whites left out, with holes and
  specks under six pixels closed.
- **Its mix:** solved from the reference blurred inside that shape only (`--smooth-mix 3`). Neighbouring strokes
  then carry nearly the same mix and the passage dries smooth. A blur that takes in the white foam beside it gives
  near-clear mixes.
- **Two passes:** the first at 0.85 of the reference on the bare band, `dry`, a look; the second on what that look
  still lacks.

```bash
python scripts/crisp_layers.py reference.png face_1.json --sheet 768,1024 --within band.png \
    --paints cobalt_blue,phthalo_green,burnt_sienna,quinacridone_rose \
    --threshold 0 --cap 5 --darkest 0.55 --smooth-mix 3 --holes 6 --sizes 8,4,2,1.2 --gain 0.85
# paint it, then the same with --look <the new look> and --gain 1.0
```

On the rehearsal the face reached 0.331 against the photograph's 0.331, where the foam layers alone left it at 0.388.
The thin white streaks across it stayed crisp, as paper, because every round kept inside the shape.

## Breaking foam

A mass of foam is one continuous light shape full of small dark gaps, and both obvious methods fail on it. Negative
painting stacks its strokes and dries dark, muddy and seamed: it works only for separate holes, as in lace. The
thirteen measured stages (`copy_reference.py --even-passages`, with the sea and sand fenced off by `--exclude`) get
the values right (gap 0.049) but dry soft and rimmed, with no crisp spray.

What worked best was crisp measured layers (`crisp_layers.py`). Each layer works from a look taken after a `dry`,
and paints only where the sheet is still lighter than the reference by more than a threshold, in shapes whose rounds
stay inside them. The whites, wherever the reference's absorbance is under 0.12 in every channel, are kept as paper.
Five layers, as threshold and cap on the mean absorbance gap:

```bash
for layer in 0.05,0.3 0.1,0.45 0.2,0.7 0.3,1.2 0.3,2.0; do
    look=$(watercolor look paintings/beach | python -c 'import json,sys; print(json.load(sys.stdin)["look"])')
    python scripts/crisp_layers.py reference.png foam.json --sheet 768,1024 --within band.png \
        --paints cobalt_blue,phthalo_green,burnt_sienna,quinacridone_rose --look "$look" \
        --threshold "${layer%,*}" --cap "${layer#*,}"
    watercolor paint paintings/beach foam.json
done
```

On a 320 by 240 crop that reached an edge agreement of 0.63 and kept the white streaks. On the full sheet the five
layers took 15,000 strokes and an hour and a half.

What it does not reach: the foam dries as a mosaic of small square-ended touches. The photograph's foam is finer than
the smallest round (about 2 pixels), so measuring it builds it from dabs. Three attempts to soften it on top all
failed:

| Tried after the layers | Result |
|---|---|
| Pale shadows inside the white foam (rose, cobalt, sienna) | At three times the computed strength the values matched (0.911 against 0.893), but they dried as lilac specks in hard-edged dabs |
| Long tapered strokes along the foam's shadow shapes | Pale, because the layers had already laid the tone; no visible change |
| One wet wash over the whole band with masked whites and darks charged in (0.8.0) | Lost all structure; see below |

## Lace

Strands of foam three to six pixels wide, lying on water, survive only as negative painting from an explicit mask
of the foam. The measured stages tone every strand narrower than their row brushes. Size every point of every stroke
from the two-dimensional distance to the nearest foam pixel, not from the height of the band of water it lies in. A
round sized to a band's height reaches sideways across the neighbouring strands where the band narrows or turns.
Painted that way, the lace stayed intact (edge agreement 0.52 on the rehearsal). The water between strands came out
slightly light, because more of the foam survived. `crisp_layers.py` sizes its rounds by the same rule, with the
foam as its lights (`--threshold 0 --cap 5`, `--lightest` set to the foam's absorbance), but it has not been run on
lace: the lace here was painted by code that walked the water in columns, not rows.

If the reference is drawn and not photographed, draw it at the painting's scale. True perspective makes the far
water between strands one or two pixels tall, and a round of size 1 lays almost nothing.

## Masking fluid (0.8.0)

Masking fluid keeps a white crisp under any wash laid over it. On 0.8.0 and 0.9 it kept only a white about 2 pixels
in radius and more. On 0.10.0 it keeps a white of any width, measured under one wash of about 0.28 and one of 0.53:

- **A line** of any radius up to 1.25 lays the same, finest fluid. It seals an unbroken band 1 pixel wide along a row
  or column through pixel centres, 2 pixels between them, and 1.3 to 1.8 at other angles. The white stays at paper
  (0.94 to 0.97) along its whole length. Wider fluid seals about one pixel less than twice its radius (radius 2, about
  3 pixels). Along a row, fluid of radius 1.5 to 2.5 varies by 2 pixels in width as the grain moves its edge, so lay
  an even fine white with the finest fluid.
- **A dot** needs a radius of 1.5 or more: 1.5 kept 4 to 6 pixels, 2 kept 6 to 9 and 2.5 kept 10 to 14, all at paper.
  A dot of 1.25 or less keeps 1 to 3 pixels. On whole-pixel coordinates it always kept its pixel; of three laid
  midway between four pixel centres, one kept none.
- **The edge** of a masked white is a hard step from paper to the wash, with no lighter rim. Mask lights whose edges
  are crisp, and paint round a light whose edge should melt into the water.

`scripts/mask_lights.py` lays one stroke of the finest fluid along each row of each light, through the pixel centres,
so the fluid covers exactly the light's pixels. On a test sheet of white lines 1 to 5 pixels wide at three angles and
dots 2 to 6 pixels across, every light pixel stayed at paper (0.96) under a wash of 0.28, unbroken, with none beside
them. It masks every light by default, and needs 0.10.0: its finest fluid is laid for the floor and the crisp edge
that version added. Paint over the fluid freely, `dry`, then `unmask`.

Two trials on the surf crop on 0.8.0, against the crisp layers above (edge agreement 0.64). The script then filled
each light with rows of rounds, and kept only whites about 2 pixels in radius and more:

| Trial | Result |
|---|---|
| Every white masked, streaks included (`--streaks 30`). The whole band laid as one wet variegated wash of rows, the darks charged in at once from a round drained to 0.6, `dry`, `unmask` | The broad foam mass came out as clean paper with a crisp edge. Almost every streak and fleck narrower than the fluid's limit was lost. The wet wash ran together into a soft gradient from grey to pink, the charged darks vanished into it, and its lower edge pooled against the dried sand as a dark line. Edge agreement 0.03. |
| Only the whites the fluid can hold masked (about 4,000 of the crop's 5,200 light pixels), then the face passes and the five crisp layers, allowed to run across the masked whites, `unmask` | The same sheet as without the fluid: value gap 0.048 and edge agreement 0.64 both times, and the same mosaic. The crisp layers already keep every white as paper, so the fluid had nothing to add, and it cost about 1,900 extra strokes. |

So leave the fluid out of surf painted with the crisp layers. It earns its place where a broad wash has to cross a
white that the wash cannot be cut round: a whitecap or a crest of foam inside a sea laid as one wash, a sail against
a sky. Lay it with `mask_lights.py`, lay the wash, `dry`, `unmask`. On 0.8.0 and 0.9 expect anything under about 2
pixels in radius to be lost. A wet wash over masked foam does not make the foam: its structure has to be painted on
dry paper.

## Lights, glints and reflections

- **Keep the lights as paper, or mask them.** On 0.10.0 opaque white over the foam's middle values dried only about
  four levels lighter. From 0.11.0 it lies over them (over a middle value of 0.67 one coat reads 0.79, two 0.85:
  `body-colour.md`), which serves for spray and late flecks, but the brightest foam still needs paper or a mask.
- **Lifting did not register on water or wet sand.** Four attempts across two studies left nothing visible. One
  was a size 3 round drained to 0.15, drawn twice along each reflection on a flat-wash sand at a film of 0.28. The
  others were blots and dry glazes. Vertical reflection streaks glazed on dry paper read as a barcode. Leave calm
  wet sand as a smooth sheen gradient.
- **Darks dropped into a fresh wash vanish.** A dark line under the swash edge and reflection streaks charged into a
  flat-wash film of about 0.8 dried invisible, and so did a size 1.8 round charged at a film of 0.25. A thin dark
  line under the swash works as a dry glaze once the sand has dried.

## Correcting a broad wash afterwards

The sand came out 0.04 lighter than the photograph after everything else was on. A glaze over it works only one
way; the general rule is in `glazing-and-economy.md`.

- On 0.10.0, with the brush at full water (0.95), the glaze stirred the dried wash into coarse speckle: the spread of
  values went from 0.042 to 0.072, and the sand darkened by only 0.012. From 0.11.0 the dried wash lies beneath the
  glaze and a full brush leaves only slight speckle (`glazing-and-economy.md`); that passage has not been repainted.
- With the brush at 0.55 there was no speckle. It laid about a quarter of the calibrated estimate, and the
  darkening grew as about the 1.45 power of the gain: 0.009 at gain 1, 0.066 at gain 4. Gain 2.8 on the full sheet
  brought the wet sand to 0.590 against 0.594.
- Mix every segment from the reference less a dry look, both unblurred and restricted to the sand. An earlier glaze
  mixed from a blur that took in the white foam came out near-clear, and its edge dried as a tide line across the
  wet sand.
- Where a segment already matches, its mix is near-clear, and the edge of that near-clear stretch dries as a
  hairline. So do not boost the sand wash near the shore if a glaze will follow.
